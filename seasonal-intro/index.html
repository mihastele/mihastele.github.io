SEASONAL

What am I exploring this season?

One season. One thing. A natural stopping point.

Read the story

Seasonal is deliberately not another productivity system.

Most tools treat curiosity as a project to be optimised. They turn hobbies into backlogs, learning into streaks, and exploration into a dashboard that tells you your hobby efficiency dropped 17%. You end up with a shelf of abandoned Week-1 tutorials and a vague sense of guilt.

Seasonal takes the opposite view. Curiosity is broad. That is not a flaw to fix. It just needs a shape: a temporary commitment, and a natural place to stop.

One season at a time

A season is a single thing you are exploring, for a set number of weeks.

Sep 28 → Nov 22
Build a Tiny PLC
Learn embedded control by actually building one.
Nov 23 → Jan 17
Learn Swedish
Up next — it waits its turn. It never ends the season before it.

Planning what comes next never overwrites what you are doing now. At most one season is active. The next one simply has a future start date.

Success is exploration, not mastery

A season is successful if you genuinely explored something — not only if you mastered it.

That single rule decides everything else. No XP. No levels. No streaks. No scores. No efficiency graph. Nothing that quietly turns your curiosity into a performance review.

No streaks

Missing a day is not a failure. There is no day to miss.

No score

A season is not a task to complete. It is time you spent.

No judgement

Nothing watches your progress and rates it. Least of all an AI.

The end-of-season ritual

When a season ends, Seasonal asks three questions. All optional. No report, no summary, no charts.

What did I make?

What did I learn?

Do I want to return to this someday?

Then it lets you go. A season can end because it is over, not because you failed it.

A quiet reminder, if you want one

A few days before a season ends, Seasonal can gently ask what you would like to explore next. You choose whether it reminds you, how many days before the end, and at what time. One nudge toward the end of a season — never a daily habit machine.

Why it exists

Seasonal started with an argument about a hypothetical app.

One of us wanted to build a place to keep every hobby, every half-finished project, every “someday”. The other pointed out that this is just a more beautiful way to feel bad about yourself. The shelf of Week-1 tutorials is not a storage problem. It is a promise problem: everything is open forever, so nothing ever feels finished.

So we changed the question. Not “what are you working on?” — that one never ends. Not “what is your goal?” — that one turns play into work. Instead:

What am I exploring this season?

A season is long enough to get somewhere and short enough to end without guilt. When it ends, you get to decide: continue, return later, or let it go. All three are fine. The point was never to become a person who finishes everything. The point was to keep exploring.

Seasonal is small on purpose. It does one thing, does it well, and then gets out of your way. Finished over featured.

The palette

Paper, clay, and a single brush stroke. Warm, handmade, calm — and never red-for-failure or green-for-success, because a season is neither.
